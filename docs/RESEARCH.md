# Diversity.ca — research and content decisions

Research date: 31 October 2026. Prepared for the website build, not as a legal opinion or an assertion that Diversity.ca holds any credential. The owner-approved master brief remains the source of truth for the proposed service offering.

## Positioning decision

Present Diversity.ca as a Canadian consulting and implementation business serving corporations and government with equal prominence. Make the buyer's next step concrete: define the problem, assess the starting point, prioritize actions, build capability and coordinate implementation. Explain deliverables before broad promises. This is a positioning recommendation inferred from the landscape below, not a claim of market exclusivity.

The useful connection across the seven divisions is the passage from advice to practical change: workforce policies, accessible experiences, procurement practices and physical improvements. Avoid an unsubstantiated claim that Diversity.ca is the only integrated provider. Show seven distinct scopes, with a shared engagement method and coordinated delivery where requested.

## Landscape: relevant primary-source examples

| Provider | What its own website establishes | Implication for Diversity.ca |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deloitte Canada | Its DEIA page describes organizational assessment, leadership learning, strategy and implementation services. [1] | An enterprise audience will expect clear methods, accountable workstreams and credible people. Do not attempt to imitate unverified scale or experience. |
| Diversio | Its consulting page presents scoped advisory engagements, policy review, action planning and longer strategic partnerships, alongside training and analytics. [2] | Specify what a client receives and how scope is agreed. Avoid vague promises that an isolated workshop changes organizational culture. |

No competitor client names, testimonials, metrics, proprietary methodologies or certifications may be reused. These examples inform information architecture, not a claim that Diversity.ca has equivalent qualifications.

## Buyer journeys and content requirements

### Corporate buyer

Recommended opening questions: What is changing in the organization? Which teams or locations are affected? Is the immediate priority workforce practices, accessibility, leadership capability, procurement or implementation? What decisions must the engagement support?

Explain deliverables such as an assessment summary, prioritized action register, ownership map, implementation sequence and measurement framework. Use outcome language carefully: “designed to support” and “help identify” are appropriate; guaranteed productivity gains, employee retention gains or financial returns are not evidenced.

### Government and public-sector buyer

Make capabilities, proposed scope, service language, accessibility of deliverables, procurement reference and submission deadline easy to provide. Separate a request for Diversity.ca's proposal from advice about the client's own procurement processes. A web enquiry is not a formal bid submission channel.

CanadaBuys is the official federal tender and award notice source. Its guidance explains that submission methods vary by opportunity. The site's government journey must never suggest that completing Diversity.ca's form registers a supplier or submits a tender to government. [3]

The CanadaBuys Buyer's Portal became the official Acquisitions Program information source on 30 January 2026; the older Supply Manual is archived. The current accessibility guidance calls for consideration of accessibility early in procurement and, where appropriate, in specifications and deliverables. [4] This supports content about accessible requirements and evaluation evidence, not a promise to determine legal compliance.

### Education and other large institutions

Show use cases relating to accessible learning environments, staff capability, campus or facility wayfinding, procurement and change management. Do not imply a school-board, university, hospital or Crown corporation is a client. Availability, professional requirements and delivery arrangements must be confirmed for each engagement.

## Seven division content guidance

The following are proposed scopes drawn from the approved brief. They describe deliverables that can be scoped, not completed client results.

| Division | Useful subservice depth | Illustrative deliverables to scope | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diversity, equity and inclusion | Organizational assessment; policy review; workforce strategy | Discovery summary, barriers and opportunities register, action plan with owners and review points | No fabricated demographic baselines, case studies or guaranteed outcomes. |
| Accessibility advisory | Digital review; built-environment assessment; planning and accommodations | Findings with evidence, prioritized remediation roadmap, responsibility matrix | An assessment is not a government certification or blanket assurance of compliance. |
| HR and organizational consulting | Culture and employee experience; leadership; change management | Listening plan, process recommendations, change and communication roadmap | Do not imply licensed legal advice or a verified clinical service. |
| Corporate training | Inclusion and accessibility education; leadership; customized workshops | Learning objectives, tailored session plan, accessible materials, follow-up evaluation | Do not promise statutory certification or credential recognition without verification. |
| Supplier diversity and procurement | Program design; supplier engagement; measurement | Baseline methodology, outreach plan, sourcing criteria, reporting measures | Diversity.ca is not presented as a certification council or supplier verification authority. |
| Social impact and sustainability | Stakeholder engagement; social procurement; impact planning | Engagement plan, outcome framework, data definitions, reporting structure | Do not offer unsupported environmental claims, assurance or guaranteed emissions reductions. |
| Accessibility solutions and implementation | Door operators; signage and wayfinding; barrier removal | Needs and site review, product options, coordinated scope, installation and handover plan | Confirm product suitability, permits and appropriately qualified installers for the actual site; no invented stock, brands or completed projects. |

## Regulatory and standards guardrails

- **Federal accessibility:** The Accessible Canada Regulations guidance distinguishes accessibility plans, feedback processes and progress reports, with consultation of people with disabilities. Applicability depends on the entity and regulator. Do not say the same obligations apply to every Canadian organization. [5]
- **Ontario web accessibility:** Ontario's guidance identifies public websites for designated public-sector organizations and businesses/non-profits with at least 50 employees, and references WCAG 2.0 AA with stated exceptions. Do not call WCAG 2.2 the universally mandated Canadian legal baseline. [6]
- **Website engineering target:** WCAG 2.2 AA is the project target. W3C publishes testable criteria, but automated checks alone cannot establish full conformance. The site should describe actual accessibility features and a feedback route without an unsupported certification badge. [7]
- **Social procurement:** PSPC's policy supports appropriate socio-economic measures and identifies its scope and limits, including distinct treatment of Indigenous procurement contexts. Avoid suggesting supplier diversity, social procurement and Indigenous procurement are interchangeable. [8]
- **Supplier certification:** Certification belongs to the relevant council and its verification process. For example, IWSCC describes eligibility and review for Veteran-Owned and Disabled-Owned businesses. Diversity.ca may advise on program design or application preparation, but must not confer or guarantee certification. [9]
- **Privacy and consent:** Present the purpose of enquiry collection at the point of collection, identify the actual service provider before enabling submission, and request only useful project information. Do not solicit employee disability details, demographic datasets, security-sensitive RFP materials or confidential files through a general lead form. Privacy text requires owner review and operational confirmation. [10]

## Content and conversion decisions

1. Use the approved descriptor, “PEOPLE | OPPORTUNITY | PROGRESS,” with “GENS | OPPORTUNITÉS | PROGRÈS” in French. The separate proposed marketing slogan remains subject to approval.
2. Give corporate and government paths comparable visibility. Service-specific calls to action should preselect the relevant service, while allowing correction.
3. Show practical methods and sample deliverables instead of invented consultant biographies or client proof. Do not publish a leadership directory until real names, biographies and permissions are supplied.
4. Use project-level intake: organization, work email, audience, service, province/territory, timeline, optional budget range and optional RFP reference/deadline. Provide a plain warning against submitting sensitive personal or procurement information.
5. Until a form provider is configured and tested, accurately explain the email fallback. Opening an email application does not mean the enquiry was sent. Do not display fake success states.
6. The website should be useful without tracking scripts. If analytics or marketing cookies are later added, reassess consent and privacy disclosures against the actual tools and jurisdictions.
7. Use national coverage language without addresses or local-team claims. Location pages must offer unique practical information; a national region selector is preferable to dozens of duplicated city pages.
8. Resources can explain how to prepare an assessment, write a useful scope or plan accessible procurement. Date them, identify the editorial author honestly, cite sources, and avoid presenting them as legal advice.
