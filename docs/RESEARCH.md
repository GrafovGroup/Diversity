## Buyer needs and proposed positioning

The brief identifies corporate and public-sector buyers as equal audiences. Their immediate website questions are practical: which problem can this service address, what work is included, what will be delivered, who must participate, and what information is needed to scope an engagement?

Proposed positioning: a direct consulting and implementation service that connects people, procurement and accessible environments. Describe each engagement through an agreed scope, evidence gathering, a usable deliverable and a handover. Disclose qualified specialist involvement when physical installation, technical inspection or regulated work requires it. These are proposed operating commitments for the owner to confirm, not evidence of completed engagements.

The navigation therefore separates corporate and government journeys, while sharing seven substantive service divisions. A request for a proposal collects audience, service, geography, timing and procurement deadline. Assessment and training enquiries use their own entry points. Pages should let a buyer understand the offer before asking them to contact the business.

## Competitive landscape: hypotheses to validate

A current named-competitor audit was not completed in this restricted session. The following are positioning hypotheses about common supplier categories, not verified comparisons with specific firms:

| Supplier category                                 | Typical buying question to investigate                                                     | Diversity.ca content response proposed for review                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Broad management consultancies                    | How does a strategic recommendation become an assigned, funded work plan?                  | Connect assessments to priorities, accountable roles and implementation planning.                              |
| Specialist inclusion or HR advisers               | How will an engagement account for procurement, accessibility and operational constraints? | Show the related divisions and define cross-functional discovery.                                              |
| Training providers                                | What changes after the workshop?                                                           | Describe practice, manager reinforcement and evaluation rather than promising transformation from attendance.  |
| Accessibility auditors and installation suppliers | Who assesses the need, specifies work and coordinates qualified delivery?                  | Separate advisory scope from product sourcing and installation management; disclose responsibility boundaries. |

Before publishing comparative claims, review current Canadian competitors directly, record their actual services and evidence, and have the owner validate differentiation. No claims of “leading,” “best,” market share, privileged procurement access or superior results are supported by this research.

## Procurement and regulatory boundaries

Current obligations depend on the buyer, jurisdiction, sector, procurement instrument and work performed. Do not say that one assessment ensures compliance with every Canadian accessibility or human-rights requirement. Ask which legislation, standard, contract condition or internal policy the buyer needs addressed, then have an appropriately qualified person confirm applicability and scope.

The CanadaBuys policies-and-guidelines page was attempted but could not be retrieved because the environment's network proxy denied its domain. Its current content was not reviewed. The following primary references remain recommended for release review, and are not represented as fetched evidence:

- [CanadaBuys policies and guidelines](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/how-procurement-works/policies-and-guidelines).
- [Accessible Canada Act](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/A-0.6/).
- [Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/05a11).
- [Québec Commission d'accès à l'information](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/) for applicable privacy guidance.
- [W3C published WCAG 2.2 Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) to compare the implemented checks against the published standard rather than relying solely on a source branch.

Procurement identifiers, supplier registrations, security clearances, insurance, certifications and professional credentials must be supplied and verified before they appear in a capability statement. The site must not imply eligibility for a standing offer, supply arrangement or preferred-supplier list without evidence.
